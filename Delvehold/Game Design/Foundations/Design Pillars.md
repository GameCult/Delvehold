---
title: Design pillars
description: The promises every system and content decision must preserve.
---

## One world, two bodies

Every material decision crosses the mode boundary through owned state. DELVE feels the workshop economy bodily; HOLD receives expedition outcomes as more than a success number.

## Preparation is play

Choosing a contract, kit, route, process, safeguard, or civic commitment should express judgement. Repeating solved menu labor should be automated or removed.

## Consequence without erasure

Retreat, injury, loss, shortage, and political defeat create new conditions for play. They hurt without deleting the evening or demanding a universal reset.

## A living target

Dungeons remember, adapt, communicate, and participate in a larger ecology. Foreign societies and institutions also act beyond player attention. The world is not a backdrop waiting for interaction.

## Legible authority

Players can tell who authored a contract, owns an object, made a claim, ratified a mandate, or reported an event. Interfaces preserve provenance, uncertainty, consent, and refusal.

## Local agency inside large systems

Players inhabit delvers and independent workshop managers. They influence civilization through work, relationships, association, and consequence rather than receiving a strategy-game throne.

## Cozy does not mean consequence-free

Warmth, mastery, Hold life, and humane rhythms are the foreground of HOLD. They matter because the workshop is embedded in scarcity, ecology, politics, and mutual dependence.
