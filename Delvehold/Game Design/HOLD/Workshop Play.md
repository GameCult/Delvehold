---
title: Workshop play
description: The daily practice, relationships, and responsibilities of one independent workshop.
---

The workshop is a durable productive practice and the bearer of one civic seal. The player begins by scrying, preparing, crafting, repairing, carrying, and tending processes directly.

Manual work teaches the material rules that later automation expresses. It should be tactile, readable, and brief enough that learning a process is satisfying without requiring permanent repetition. The workshop grows through new practices, tools, assistants, relationships, automation patterns, and connections to shared institutions.

No workshop efficiently contains every production chain. A player chooses what to automate, commission, trade, and request through a contract. Specialization and recurring collaborators create dependence without placing workshops on a shared city map.

The workshop identity survives absence. Its active [[Game Design/Shared World/World Graph|Hold attachment]] expires after 36 hours without an authenticated foreground session, stopping bounded automation and removing the workshop from live local circulation. Returning immediately restores the same practice and history in the former Hold or attaches it to another one. Absence does not decay tools, erase relationships, or multiply political authority.

The cozy fantasy comes from accumulated familiarity: named stations, remembered materials, recurring customers and delvers, repaired tools, small rituals, and a machine whose history remains visible. Efficiency is useful, but the workshop is not reduced to throughput.
