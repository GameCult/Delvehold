---
title: Production and logistics
description: Facilities, recipes, capacity, custody, and specialization across Holds.
---

Production transforms identified inputs through a facility and practice into owned outputs. Every transformation records recipe or method, consumed and produced lots, operator or automation, facility, time, quality-relevant conditions, and provenance.

Facilities expose bounded capacity. Reservations, maintenance, power, labor, route access, storage, and safety can constrain that capacity without becoming disconnected modifier cards. Logistics moves custody through workshops, institutions, contracts, and inter-Hold routes; inventory is not globally available because a number exists somewhere.

No workshop efficiently contains every chain. Players specialize, trade, commission, use institutional services, or accept less efficient substitutes. Orders express desired future exchange; accepted orders become commitments; fulfilled transfers become realized trade. Only realized effects enter the aggregate external-world boundary.

Workshop reattachment does not teleport committed material. Private configuration and uncommitted inventory may resume with the workshop identity; escrowed, dispatched, installed, institution-held, or otherwise transferred lots remain under their recorded custody until another receipted transfer moves them.

The economy must support ordinary civic life as well as expedition gear: heat, food processing, medicine, construction, transport, maintenance, education, ritual goods, and delightful nonsense all compete for material and capacity.
