title: Extraction play
description: Contracted expeditions where the quota, the party, and the dungeon all exert pressure.
---

DELVE is an extraction RPG because every expedition is organized around a return decision.

A HOLD player publishes a contract with a target, offered equipment, promised compensation, and desired materials. A DELVE party enters a procedurally generated dungeon carrying that economic intent into an ecology that can answer it.

## The expedition tension

During a run, the party repeatedly chooses among:

- satisfying the contracted quota;
- pursuing an unexpected discovery;
- recovering a fallen delver or lost equipment;
- preserving scarce supplies;
- retreating before the dungeon's response escalates;
- pressing deeper for a return that may transform both clients.

Extraction is successful when something meaningful returns, not only when the original number is met. Injuries, maps, creature observations, evidence of core behaviour, broken equipment, and expedition history are persistent outputs alongside material.

## The living target

The dungeon is not a loot table around combat rooms. It remembers pressure, adapts to recurring techniques, moves valuables, develops counter-organisms, and may learn the signatures of particular workshops or parties.

Because [[Dungeon Lifecycle|bounded delving]] is part of its life, the core permits and produces opportunity while resisting intrusion. The extraction field is therefore neither a passive mine nor a moral patient waiting to be left untouched. It is a participant whose goals are only partly legible.

## The return stroke

HOLD players process what returns and live with what does not. Equipment lost below becomes dungeon matter. A successful material stream enables automation and raises future demand. A discovered ecological warning may be worth more than the quota if anyone believes it.

The two modes meet in this argument over what the expedition was for.
