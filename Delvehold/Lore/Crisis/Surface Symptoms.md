---
title: Surface symptoms
description: How disturbance beneath dwarven mines spreads into distant climates, ecologies, and settlements.
---

Disturbance beneath dwarven mines can reach the surface and spread far beyond the workings. Early extraction failures can be contained by sealing a mine gate. As workings go deeper, the trouble reaches weather, rivers, fields, and settlements in foreign territory.

Records describe several kinds of surface change:

- New dungeons appear, and old ones suddenly expand.
- Monster migration intensifies.
- Mana storms occur, and magical weather becomes unstable.
- Forests mutate, and root-cavern systems become physically connected.
- Rivers change course without an ordinary geological cause.
- Crops mutate, magical pests appear, and livestock develop abnormalities.
- Earthquakes occur, and ancient spirits wake far from any active mine.

## Attribution

An individual event does not establish that these disturbances belong to a planetary system. Climate, magic, geology, local cores, sabotage, and ordinary variation remain plausible explanations for particular cases.

The broader case depends on comparing records: when events happened, where they occurred, whether disturbances repeatedly affected the same kinds of functions, and whether mana changed alongside them. The pattern matters because several independent sources describe changes that line up.

Elven maps predict that surface effects will follow deep mana currents. Years later, halfling field books locate crop failures along those predicted lines. Goblin route logs date the first underground changes to the same weeks as those failures. Dwarven models can dismiss any one of these archives. They have more difficulty accounting for the overlap.

## Political consequence

When damage crosses a border, the operation of a dwarven mine becomes a foreign-policy dispute. Downstream councils send ruined seed and veterinary reports to their capitals. Those governments must respond to the damage, but they have no vote over the machinery responsible.

The evidence feeds into tariffs, military estimates, and speeches preparing the public for war. Decisions made beneath a dwarven mine can leave distant governments weighing military action.
