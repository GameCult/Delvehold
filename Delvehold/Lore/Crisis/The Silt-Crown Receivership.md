---
title: The Silt-Crown receivership
description: A wounded watershed core uses bearer debt to take control of the company drilling into it.
---

Silt-Crown is an awakened regional [[Dungeon Cores|core]] distributed through the springs, buried rivers, silt beds, and crystal seams of a watershed beyond the Greathold's western marches. Its flooded chambers lie on both sides of three surface borders. Nobody agrees whether those chambers are one body, several allied cores, or only the parts of a deeper system presently willing to answer to one name. The uncertainty did not stop Seven Weirs Deep Company from leasing a cutting face inside it.

## The Lower Course bonds

Seven Weirs financed its descending rail, flood pumps, and first three bores by issuing **Lower Course bonds**: bronze bearer plates divided into ten breakable coupon teeth. Hold reserve houses, human pension halls, Great Houses, and temple treasuries bought them because the same company supplied crystal to municipal pumps abroad and winter heat along [[The Greathold|the Greathold]].

The covenant was written for an ordinary failed mine. After two missed quarterly deliveries, bearers holding more than half the unredeemed plate could appoint a **continuance receiver**. Until arrears were cured, that receiver would control the mine plan, payroll, contracted rail allotments, ore sales, and any new borrowing needed to keep the concern alive. The clause protected creditors from an incompetent owner. It said nothing about a creditor being the ground beneath the owner.

Silt-Crown acquired the plates without minting a coin. It opened low-risk delving routes to selected goblin route-houses, placed mature crystals and old surface treasure where bounded parties could take them, and communicated preferred purchases by arranging chambers in the shapes stamped on bond prospectuses. The goblin factors sold the returns through halfling markets and bought frightened holders' plates at a discount. The [[Gods and Religion|Temple of the Open Scale]] assayed each bearer and asked no question its own contract did not require.

Once Silt-Crown held six tenths of the surviving principal, passages closed ahead of two bores and Seven Weirs missed its deliveries. The factors presented the plates. The Open Scale appointed its under-reeve as receiver and discovered that refusing the claim would discredit every bearer covenant the commerce cult had blessed.

## A mine commanded by its wound

The receiver cancelled direct drainage into Silt-Crown's spawning galleries, restored paid [[Dungeon Lifecycle|delving]] for removal from contested chambers, and assigned company pumps to return clean water to damaged tributaries. Mineworkers kept their wages and gained enforceable withdrawal thresholds. Output fell, municipal buyers entered rationing, and junior bondholders stopped receiving interest.

Silt-Crown cannot simply close the concern. The receiver must preserve the mine as a going concern and service the very debt that grants control. The core spends old treasure and ecological reserves to maintain its majority while authorizing enough extraction to pay crews, rails, and coupons. Some of that work is displaced into neighbouring organs whose relation to Silt-Crown is no clearer than a Hold's relation to the [[Nested Dungeons|mountain beneath it]]. A dungeon has gained leverage over industry by becoming responsible for part of it.

## The tangle of recognition

Seven Weirs calls the closure creditor sabotage and argues that treasure taken from its lease bought the bonds with stolen company property. Silt-Crown's factors answer that a body does not sabotage a blade by bleeding on it. The dispute cannot be resolved without deciding where the watershed core's body begins, whether its arranged chambers constitute instruction, and whether goblin factors served a client, a neighbour, or their own investment syndicate.

The interested powers divide without forming clean camps:

- Great Houses need the Lower Course covenant honoured because their own capital rests on comparable debt, but fear any precedent that lets the land beneath an old claim become its senior creditor.
- Forge Consortiums defend bearer law while funding surveys that define a core as only its central crystal organ. On that map, most mine closures become attacks by an external actor rather than a wounded creditor's refusal.
- Rune College engineers, elven jurists, and goblin route witnesses can make a wider body legible, although they disagree over whether financial standing is recognition or merely a profitable substitute for personhood.
- Orcish treaty-keepers argue that old watershed obligations rank ahead of every bond. Human ministries whose pensions hold junior plates demand full output while their river towns demand repair.
- The Open Scale priesthood cannot renounce its receiver without weakening commerce-temple credit, yet every instruction it translates makes a temple official the public voice of a core the law still will not admit as a person.
- The mine unions support the withdrawal thresholds and oppose repayment plans that return Seven Weirs to control. District kitchens, rail offices, and foreign pump towns form the constituency for faster output, even when they accept that the old mine plan was an injury.

Lower Course plates mature in eight years. Seven Weirs and its allies mean to tender the face value, redeem the bearer plates, and end the receivership before the core can turn contractual control into a treaty. Silt-Crown must obtain recognized standing while its debt still speaks for it, or acquire the successor financing without becoming the permanent engine of the extraction it resisted.

## Open pressures

- If the company proves that some bond money came from treasure inside its lease, does that make Silt-Crown a thief, the mine's unpaid supplier, or the owner the lease failed to name?
- Can a creditor cause default by refusing injury to its own body, and which local, regional, or watershed boundary answers that question?
- If Silt-Crown runs a useful concern, pays workers, and answers complaints through a receiver, has it founded a workshop eligible for [[Mastership and Citizenship|a civic seal]], or only learned to wear a temple as a legal hand?
