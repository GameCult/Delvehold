---
title: Open work
description: Unfinished spell patterns traded through shared mana routes before a terminator makes their effect physical.
---

An **open working** is a spell engine process divided across distance. A supplier's rune bank establishes and stabilizes most of a magical pattern, then sends the [[Mana and Pattern|patterned mana]] into a leased conduit without giving it an effect-producing terminator. The buyer's terminal engine reads the arriving history, draws the working quantity of mana, and makes the intended effect physical.

The practice turns part of a spell into freight. A pumping house can own sound engines and a winter stock of crystals while importing the unfinished pattern that tells its terminators how to lift water. Rail traction, refrigeration, mine pressure, ward coordination, and furnace control all move this way where a remote rune bank is cheaper than a local body of practitioners.

Long open workings use gold-filled or carefully layered conduits to preserve their history. The largest runs follow [[The Greathold|the Greathold's]] ancient spine routes beside rail, lift, and ventilation infrastructure. Shared branches cannot admit unrelated workings carelessly: their histories combine before the split. Route offices therefore sell uninterrupted **spell paths**, measured in conduit length, pressure, and time, and junction crews close each path with the null rune before opening the next.

## The Last-Stroke Doctrine

Old workshop law held that an apprentice carrying an unfinished rune graph between Masters had neither delivered a product nor cast a spell. Responsibility settled when a final maker inspected the accumulated work, added the terminator, and allowed the effect into the world. Industrial courts preserved this rule as the **Last-Stroke Doctrine**.

Each Hold may charge for labour and rune stages performed inside it, while its route office charges for conduit capacity. Product duty, effect restrictions, and ordinary liability fall where the terminator closes. Forge Consortiums exploit the division by placing expensive rune stages in low-duty Holds, declaring the intervening flow unfinished work, and closing it where a municipality offers an industrial exemption. No single route ledger contains the value of the whole working, because much of that value is a sequence owned as consortium practice rather than a physical cargo.

The doctrine also gives foreign buyers a dangerous kind of independence. A town may import the pattern but burn its own crystals at the terminator, allowing both sides to claim that no fuel crossed the border. During the [[International Crisis|international crisis]], customs houses begin null-closing open workings at frontier junctions. The seizure leaves crystal stores, pumps, and rails visibly intact while water, refrigeration, and traction stop.

## Meaning loss

A mana meter can prove that the contracted quantity reached a terminal without proving that the promised history survived the route. Insurers call corruption, unauthorized branching, premature closure, and substituted rune stages **meaning loss**. Standard machinery policies exclude it: a terminal that faithfully makes the wrong effect has not broken, and a null-closed delivery has not lost measurable mana.

Coverage requires witness terminations at named junctions. Each witness draws off and closes a small branch into a cheap proof object whose form records what the accumulated pattern would have done there. Witnessing consumes route capacity and mana, reveals part of a proprietary working, and proves nothing about alterations made after the last witness. Consortium-owned routes can afford dense witnesses and obtain insurance; independent workshops usually accept the exclusion or buy access to the consortium's sealed path.

Municipal contracts deepen the lock. The cheapest open workings test for a maker-marked terminal before they continue, so a local enchanter can maintain the endpoint without reproducing or redirecting the imported practice. This extends the displacement already produced by [[Runic Engineering]]: technicians become expert in keeping one vendor's last rune available while losing the craft that preceded it. If the upstream rune bank closes, the district cannot substitute labour for service even while qualified mages, fuel, and machinery remain nearby.

Redundancy means duplicating the hidden rune stages, not merely stockpiling crystals. That cost directs public buyers toward longer supply contracts and directs independent Holds toward deeper extraction to pay for a second working they hope never to use. [[Dwarven Industry|Industrial dependence]] therefore grows through instructions as well as fuel.

Open work also lays persistent magical intention across structures older than the industry using them. Junction attacks attributed to [[Ecological Responses|adaptive dungeon activity]] increasingly select spell paths serving pumps, wards, and weapons before those effects occur. Engineers disagree whether the attackers follow leakage, repeated maintenance traffic, or the unfinished pattern itself. The same routes through which [[Machine-Dungeon|the machine-dungeon]] might learn its own needs may be teaching the deeper system what the Greathold intends to do next.

## Open pressures

- If an open working persists, corrects itself, and negotiates route faults across several Holds, is it cargo, a consortium's remote labour, or a new mind entitled to attempt [[Mastership and Citizenship|Mastership]]?
- When foreign customs terminates an imported working into a harmless proof object, has it inspected a service, manufactured the forbidden good, or destroyed property outside its jurisdiction?
- Are deep creatures striking open-work junctions because those junctions are fragile, or because the dungeon can read an intended pump, ward, or weapon before the last rune makes it physical?
