Older Greathold pumps, lift drives, rail brakes, and district heaters are built with one thumbnail-wide strip of copper left unvarnished. A pale blue-green lichen grows across it in hard, fine layers. Rune technicians call the growth **workskin** and the exposed metal beneath it the **witness seam**.

Workskin descends from mana-eating growths found in old Vault drains. Workshops selected strains that remain on copper, tolerate ordinary heat, and consume the trace mana escaping from joints without biting into a healthy main flow. The escaping mana arrives with the history described by [[Mana and Pattern|mana theory]]. Microscopic runic organs in the lichen change as they digest it, so repeated pump cycles, brake applications, reversals, null terminations, and overloads leave different colours and ridges in the next layer.

A skin is not a transcript and cannot identify intention. It records recurrence, sequence, and unusual strain well enough that a reader who knows the engine can distinguish ordinary wear from a pump driven beyond its rated head or a brake receiving commands after isolation. The lichen also consumes patterned leakage before conductive housings and common returns carry it onward. Kept correctly, it is a small living sink between one engine's memory and the next rune in the network.

## Keeping a skin

Every local strain has preferences. Warm-sea Holds keep witness seams in the open air; dry foundry Holds feed them through damp clay wicks. A healthy skin costs a little leakage, mineral wash, trimming, and a technician who knows the difference between brown dormancy and a dead colony. Neglect lets it overgrow a junction and become the fault it was meant to reveal.

At shift handover, a technician checks the pressure marks and then reads the skin. Important layers are copied as damp-paper **skin pulls** before trimming. The pull goes into the service book under the maintaining workshop's seal, tying an ordinary biological trace to the answerability expected under [[Mastership and Citizenship|Mastership]]. Market buyers ask to see the skin before the gauge. A machine polished for sale is called **white-faced**; the polite meaning is unproven, and the usual meaning is that somebody wants the past gone.

New engines are seeded from a workshop's slate-grown mother patch rather than from the machine they replace. Giving a journeyworker a first viable shaving is a modest professional courtesy. Refusing one after a quarrel is not formally a sanction, but it can leave an independent repairer unable to establish a locally readable witness seam for a season.

## The clean-box dispute

Forge Consortiums increasingly sell sealed, self-cleaning housings. They lose less mana, travel well between Holds, exclude aggressive moulds, and put diagnosis inside standardized arrays serviced by the manufacturer. Their warranties commonly classify any living growth inside the housing as contamination.

Independent workshops answer that a proprietary diagnostic can be reset, withheld, or rendered unreadable when a supplier closes its foreign office. Workskin remains beside the people who breathed the pump air. Scraping it leaves a clean scar; bleaching before a sale, claim, or public inspection is treated by many local moots as reason to distrust the service book even where no written rule forbids it.

The dispute reaches beyond repair custom. Replacing skinned engines with clean boxes removes thousands of small sinks from the connected [[Machine-Dungeon|industrial network]] and replaces locally held evidence with manufacturer-held testimony. Engineers do not yet know whether this changes the Greathold's awakening risk. They do know that the first warning of several expensive faults was a colour on an old pump that its new replacement is designed never to grow.

Workskin complicates the clean division between industry and the ecology resisting it. The same broad family of mana-eating moulds appears among [[Ecological Responses|industrial adaptations]] and [[Monsters|dungeon organisms]]; one branch attacks standardized machinery, while another has become part of how [[Runic Engineering|rune technicians]] keep that machinery safe. Sterilizing every engine may suppress a hazard, destroy a witness, and sever a working relationship in the same stroke.

## Open questions

- If distant skins grow the same undocumented band before a network anomaly, are they separately recording one shared history or passing a pattern between colonies?
- Who owns a skin pull that proves a public engine was overdriven: the sealed workshop, the workers who kept the colony, the Hold whose pump it was, or the manufacturer whose warranty forbade the witness?
- What must a town do when its workskin begins moving away from safe leakage before the instruments detect anything wrong?
