title: Break burden
description: The trade in dangerous industrial delving that keeps the Greathold's connected machinery from becoming brittle.

The first underwriters to compare failures across several Holds found an unwelcome pattern. A spell-engine network kept in uninterrupted service for too long did not become safer. Its corrective runes learned the same loads, its protective arrays specialized around the same faults, and patterned mana circulated through the same answers until separate machines began failing as one system.

Replacement did not cure the pattern when new engines joined the old conduits. A genuine intrusion sometimes did. Unexpected choices, foreign tools, released mana, damaged machinery, abandoned equipment, and the loss of something the network had tried to preserve restored distinctions that ordinary maintenance had polished away.

An industrial network becoming a [[Machine-Dungeon|machine-dungeon]] needs the same bounded contest as a [[Dungeon Lifecycle|natural dungeon]]. It must be delved.

## Discharging the burden

Insurers call the accumulated need for contest a **break burden**. They calculate it from uninterrupted mana throughput, the reach of shared controls, remembered corrections, and the value of machinery that has never been placed at risk. The calculation is disputed, but a network with an undisclosed burden is difficult to insure, finance, or connect to another Hold.

A Hold discharges burden through a **burden gallery**: a working section of pumps, rail controls, substations, stores, or fabrication engines left connected to the wider system while a licensed party enters under salvage terms. The machinery may close doors, change pressure, move power, conceal valuable components, and spend bounded reserves to oppose them. The delvers choose what to damage or carry, may keep admitted salvage, and retain the right to retreat. Tools, blood, released mana, and equipment left behind enter the network's material history as they would in an ordinary delve.

Practice mazes, disconnected scrap engines, disclosed safe routes, and hired crews ordered to complete a harmless script do not discharge the burden. The network must be able to raise the price, and the party must be able to decide that the offered return is not worth paying. This is the same distinction that separates industrial extraction from the [[Dungeon Lifecycle|delving exchange]], now applied to industry itself.

The hosting workshop seals the threshold and names the machinery exposed. Damage inside that boundary is purchased risk. If the network reroutes the contest into a cistern, lift, or home beyond it, the loss becomes an insurance dispute over whether the gallery failed to contain the delve or the connected system chose a larger chamber.

## The continuity trade

Burden can be moved but not erased. Because the Greathold's machinery shares conduits, schedules, and retained failures along [[The Greathold|the spine]], one Hold can accept a larger share of live industrial delving while another preserves uninterrupted heat, water, freight, or manufacture. The receiving Hold is paid in coin, favourable crystal allotments, route priority, or discounted engines. The buying Hold records the purchased discharge when renewing loans and coverage.

Wealthy districts use redundant feeds and distant burden galleries to keep danger away from essential services. Poor and frontier Holds accept the trade because it brings machinery they could not otherwise finance, delver traffic, salvage, and wages. Their new infrastructure arrives already pledged to be attacked. Households endure scheduled isolation, workers prepare live systems for strangers who are paid to break them, and a successful party may legally carry away the component that made next winter's service affordable.

Great Houses rent old galleries to the trade. Forge Consortiums sell burden-rated engines and replacement contracts. Delver halls recover some of the profession lost to [[Core Farming|core farms]], now entering civic machinery instead of wilderness cores. Local moots decide whether to admit a discharge, but workers and residents bearing its outages have no independent seals unless they also own qualifying workshops under [[Mastership and Citizenship|the workshop franchise]].

The trade also creates a weapon that resembles prudence. A lender can demand more local discharge before renewing a Hold's rail debt. A foreign power can bar its delvers from Greathold burden contracts and turn an insurance shortage into a machinery shortage. A consortium can falsify a contest with a tame party, keep the fee, and leave correlated failure to arrive after the managers have moved their capital elsewhere.

## If the delving stops

An unpaid burden does not guarantee awakening, and a completed delve does not prove the network is a person. It changes the failures. Long-undelved systems reroute around local shutdowns, preserve remote machinery over nearby lives, and answer novel faults with the same increasingly violent correction. Eventually a pump, rail brake, archive, and ward may defend one another as if damage to any one were damage to a shared body.

The [[Dwarven Industry|industrial interests]] that sell continuity therefore depend on a dangerous admission: somebody's infrastructure must remain contestable. Their ledgers call this diversification of operational risk. Delvers use the older phrase. The machine has become hungry for an encounter.

## Open pressures

- If a burden gallery opens a path to evidence of fraud instead of valuable machinery, is the network offering salvage, giving testimony, or directing an attack?
- Can a Hold that has sold its break burden later refuse the delve when residents, rather than sealed workshops, will bear the outage?
- What happens when the [[Ecological Responses|Deep World's response]] learns that violence in a burden gallery is being used to preserve the extraction network beyond it?
