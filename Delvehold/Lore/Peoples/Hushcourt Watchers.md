---
title: Hushcourt Watchers
description: A goblin neighbourhood where discovering what a mimic does unwatched can make a person famous.
---

*Canon candidate.*

In Hushcourt, a goblin can become famous for watching a bucket all night. The bucket must eventually do something. When Vessa described one creeping under a ledge and unfolding a pale, jointed underside, neighbours crowded her doorway to see the drawing. For weeks children attempted the movement on their backs. Nobody agreed how many legs she had seen.

Hushcourt occupies a dry shelf above the damp chambers of an active dungeon. Its [[Goblins|goblin]] households grow fungi, sort reusable objects brought from below, and repair baskets and carrying frames. Their small market attracts [[Adventurers|delvers]] who want provisions before going farther in. Among the residents are watchers: enthusiasts who spend their free evenings finding creatures that imitate ordinary objects, then trying to catch them doing something beyond waiting for prey.

The local [[Monsters|mimics]] can hold the shapes of small containers and loose fittings. Watchers want to discover where they go between ambushes, whether the same individual changes disguises, and what happens when two meet. They venture into unused side chambers with shaded lamps and drawing boards. A fine observation earns invitations to speak, requests for sketches, and rivals who suddenly take great interest in the observer's washing basket. The wonder is durable: a handle can loosen into a limb while someone is still deciding whether a shadow moved.

Most residents never become serious watchers. Some prefer the loud retellings in the market to an evening spent suppressing a sneeze. Children practise spotting substitutions among safe household objects; a convincing false sighting is a respectable joke until someone starts packing for an expedition. Sellers paint famous encounters on baskets. Buyers particularly like the one showing Vessa asleep beside her alleged discovery, although she maintains this never happened.

## What a boast leaves out

A watcher usually reveals the creature's appearance and behaviour before revealing the chamber. This gives a discovery time to belong to its finder. Friends receive directions privately; a respected rival may be invited along because their reluctant agreement will travel farther than a friend's praise. Locations eventually spread through sketches, whispered corrections, and people following people who insist they are merely stretching their legs.

Showing where a creature lives can spoil the watching. Lamps, food scraps, and visitors change what it encounters. Hunters may arrive with a sack. Yet secrecy also protects invented discoveries, and someone who always has a reason to go alone soon acquires an audience waiting for a mistake.

Watchers draw their sharpest distinction at bait. Watching a creature approach food is acceptable if the food appears in the account. Concealing bait to claim a spontaneous encounter is disgraceful. It is also difficult to prove. Rell, a basket mender celebrated for seeing two mimics exchange disguises, refuses to show the approach to his chamber. Vessa has found grease on his lamp case. He says he eats while waiting. She does too.

Rell's younger sister openly arranges bowls and scraps to coax mimics into view. Her demonstrations earn money from visiting delvers, and she wants to discover what the creatures can learn. Rell calls them tricks. She asks how he knows. Their disagreement has split an audience that once admired them together: some want an untouched secret life, others want an encounter they can take part in.

One small circle calls the mimics the dungeon's disguises and treats their movements as messages. Most watchers find these interpretations unhelpful for locating another animal. A creature responding to a bowl proves little about a dungeon's intentions; dungeon sentience remains a disputed fringe question beyond such local beliefs.

## Unresolved hooks

- Vessa can expose Rell's hidden feeding place, but reaching it requires crossing the recess where her own still-unannounced discovery waits.
- A visitor offers to buy the animal from the siblings' childhood favourite drawing. Neither sibling has established that it was ever the same animal on successive nights.
- Two watchers independently sketch a bucket-shaped mimic carrying a smaller one. Their accounts differ on whether it was feeding, rescuing it, or simply moving past; both have already promised a public telling.
