---
title: The Harrowind Coast
description: A candidate human coastal culture that chooses its common voyages by trying rival ambitions at sea.
---

On the Harrowind Coast, people argue about the next great voyage while standing inside unfinished boats. A shipwright can move a bench to show how many passengers her design will carry. Her rival can ask those passengers to get out again and show how much weight his narrower hull saves. Spectators climb between them, testing seats and taking sides. By launch day, half the town has acquired an opinion about a boat it cannot afford.

Harrowind is a chain of small, predominantly [[Humans|human]] port republics on a western coast, where wooded river valleys interrupt steep, windy headlands. The valleys supply timber and hemp; sheltered coves support fishing, boatyards, and smokehouses. Farmers sell grain downhill and buy cured fish for winter. Most boats spend their lives within sight of shore. Their owners may be fiercely attached to a particular cove and thoroughly uninterested in discovering another one.

Yet these towns share an ambition: once in a generation, to send a vessel farther than their parents went and bring its company home. Children grow up knowing the last voyage's landfalls, imitating its quarrels in quay-side plays, and demanding the part of the navigator who lost his trousers. Return celebrations begin when the lookout recognizes the sail. People run out of workshops carrying whatever was in their hands.

The shared ship begins with rival short voyages. Anyone able to assemble a crew can propose what the coast should attempt, then spend a summer testing some part of it. Fishing crews lend spare days; prosperous households supply canvas; a persuasive stranger sometimes borrows a hull. After each return, crews show their damage, demonstrate what worked, and answer questions on the beach. People shift their help between projects. A clever speech attracts spectators; a second successful departure attracts provisions.

Eventually the harbour gatherings must choose which attempt gets the common ship. Adult residents choose by standing with its crew, including those who will stay ashore. The towns each bring their chosen proposal to a joint gathering and repeat the choice until one has a majority of towns behind it. This can take several summers. Meanwhile fishing and ordinary coastal journeys continue. The shared vessel is paid for from a portion of harbour dues saved across generations; supporting towns combine their portions, while towns that refuse keep theirs for a later attempt.

A losing crew often joins the winning expedition with its own question still alive. A navigator who wanted a northern passage might sail south to test her method of finding position beyond familiar landmarks. People admire someone who can make an opponent's voyage succeed without pretending to have wanted it. Others call that surrender and keep sailing their own boats. Neither choice ends a person's standing in the town.

Command aboard belongs to the chosen captain; the crew chooses a replacement if that captain dies or becomes incapable. At home, captains return to ordinary standing. A celebrated explorer cannot requisition somebody's fishing boat. When a return account is disputed, the dissatisfied sailors tell their version before the same harbour crowds that backed the voyage. A captain who abandoned a companion may find every yard refusing the next commission. That public reckoning can also be cruel: a hesitant speaker has difficulty undoing a story already being sung.

Sailors visit [[Gods and Religion|shrines]] for favourable weather and safe return, often approaching several gods according to need. Some would happily carry a priest in place of another barrel of water. Their captains generally insist on the barrel as well. Returning [[Adventurers|delvers]] sometimes join as guards or expedition companions; skill underground earns attention, followed by an undignified first afternoon learning to stay upright.

The next voyage has divided Harrowind. Nessa, a pilot, wants to cross the uncharted western water. Oren, whose fast boat carried his wife home in time to see her dying sister, wants to reach a familiar distant coast and return in half the usual season. He dreams of families visiting one another without losing a year. Nessa accuses him of making a generation's great voyage into a delivery run. His trial crew has begun selling little model boats with room for passengers.

- Nessa's best navigator has bought one of Oren's models and placed it where visitors can see it.
- A small harbour has backed the western crossing, but its shipwright doubts that either trial hull will survive the open sea. She has asked both rivals to help build a third.
- A sailor from the previous great voyage is preparing to interrupt its anniversary play with an account nobody has heard before.
