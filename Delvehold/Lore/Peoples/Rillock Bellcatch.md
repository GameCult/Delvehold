# Rillock Bellcatch

On match evenings in Rillock, spectators go quiet to hear whether a falling bell rings. A catcher slides beneath it, catches the bronze mouth in one palm and pins the clapper with the other. Silence wins a point. The cheering usually makes up for it.

Rillock is a small market town among grain farms and sheep pasture, with a stream running between its cloth yards and its uphill houses. Most inhabitants are [[Humans]], earning their living from cloth finishing, livestock sales and the surrounding farms. The two oldest bellcatch clubs, Streamside and High Street, draw players from both sides of the water. Allegiance commonly follows whoever first took you to a match. Marriage does not reliably improve it.

The court is a long strip of packed sand with wooden boards around its edge. A team fields a caster and two catchers. Using the levitation taught among the useful effects of [[Runic Engineering|spellcraft]], the caster holds two small handbells a little above head height over marked spots, then lets them fall one at a time. Each bell has its own maintained working. The catchers move freely below; a bell must fall clear of magic before a catch counts. Lifting it again spoils the attempt. A round contains six drops, and the opposing team then takes the same court and bells.

The practical limits of [[Mana and Pattern|maintained spells]] shape the performance. The caster must keep the bells steady while watching their partners. Catchers read shoulders, fingers and familiar hesitations. A good trio makes the pauses entertaining: a runner pretends to lose the bell, turns away, and catches it behind one hip. An unsuccessful imitation produces a noise the whole town understands.

Children play with wooden cups dropped from a fence. Adults who never learn magic can become celebrated catchers, and some casters remain seated throughout a round. Experienced players lend their bells for small matches. The clubs pay for sand and replacement equipment through match collections and exhibition fees at livestock fairs. Tailors take particular pride in sleeves that permit a full reach without exposing quite everything.

Ordinary rounds reward clean catches. The annual Streamside–High Street match also allows one crossing catch per team for two extra points: both bells fall together, and each catcher takes the bell on the other side. That brief exchange, bodies passing and bronze descending, is what visitors come to see. It is also where Rillock's present quarrel begins.

Nessa Vale, Streamside's quickest catcher, struck her partner's knee during a crossing in spring. Her wrist healed crooked. She can still catch on her own side, but cannot reliably turn the second hand inward while running across. She wants Streamside to field her in the ordinary rounds and surrender the crossing bonus. Their caster, Oren, wants to enter her younger sister instead. He has spent three summers building a team that can finally beat High Street, and resents being asked to abandon its best sequence a week before the match.

Nessa's sister Pella has been practising the crossing with her partner after the yard closes. She wants the place. She also wants Nessa to watch her win, and has not found a way to ask. High Street's captain has offered to drop the bonus from this year's match. Oren heard pity in the offer; several High Street supporters heard an attempt to make their own victory less impressive.

Rillock's casters cannot reliably catch a falling bell with a fresh spell in the time available. The court uses small bells and deep sand, but neither prevents a collision. Catches near the boards already draw the loudest applause. If the clubs refuse the crossing, fair exhibitors may still pay pairs to perform it, taking the best runners into smaller yards with worse footing. If they keep it, Pella will be asked to make her first public crossing in the loudest match of the year.

## Hooks

- Pella asks Nessa to help rehearse the crossing. Her partner has begun arriving late and blaming work; he has bruises he will not show Oren.
- High Street's captain must announce the scoring before the visitors arrive. Streamside supporters are already selling ribbons embroidered with the old maximum score.
- A fair exhibitor offers Nessa paid solo performances during the match weekend. She could earn more than the winning purse, but would miss Pella's first appearance.
