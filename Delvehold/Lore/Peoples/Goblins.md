title: Goblins
description: Liminal dungeon communities with practical knowledge industrial engineering repeatedly underestimates.

Goblins commonly inhabit active dungeons without being wholly controlled by them. Their communities consume waste, move material, cultivate fungi, manage dangerous parasites, repair passages, and redistribute magical objects. These relationships can be exploitative, negotiated, symbiotic, or several at once.

Scholars compare goblins to a dungeon's microbial ecology. Goblins generally consider the comparison insulting, especially when delivered by someone who has never survived a moult season.

## Displacement

Core farms erase or enclose goblin habitat. Deep Companies treat occupied chambers as infestations or unauthorized settlements. Displacement initially attracts little geopolitical concern because goblin testimony is not admitted to the institutions making extraction policy.

## Practical intelligence

Generations underground produce exceptional knowledge of core moods, safe routes, mana cycles, monster migration, structural warning signs, and architectural intent. Goblin observers often know a dungeon is changing long before a runic survey produces a report.

That knowledge makes later sabotage precise. Some networks feed information to dungeon defenders, redirect monsters through industrial sites, or teach cores how standardized machinery fails. Company reports describe the result as coordinated monster attack and omit the goblin logistics that selected the targets.

### Route-songs

*Authored elaboration for the Ghostlight fixture `veil-cinderhook-route-song-v0`.*

Along the Greathold spine, several goblin guide networks keep a changing song for every passage they use often enough to know. One singer gives the road's opening cadence. Returned runners answer with new verses made from draught direction, seep taste, fungal timing, creature traffic, echoes, and the way mana settles on carried metal. Held beats mark missing witnesses; a borrowed refrain carries news from a neighbouring route. Copying the words while flattening the pauses produces a tidy report with its confidence stripped out.

A route-song predicts the next behaviour of a passage under a named pressure. At Cinderhook Relay, for example, the Glassvein verse warns that the eastern throat will pinch after a third repetition of the same engine pulse, while unpowered walkers may still have one crossing through the Low Echo culvert. It does not predict a collapse at the third bell. A delayed train can still perform the fatal repetition; a clock-bound survey may call the song false until the stone closes on a schedule its instruments never measured.

Keeping the song current costs crossings. Runners must return through shifting ground, and the chorus must compare accounts before fear or bravado hardens into a verse. A death leaves more than grief: the next performance carries an audible gap where that runner's observations should have been. Displacement ruins prediction by separating singers from the roads they maintained.

Guides commonly sell or exchange the **road refrain**, which names a usable window, the pressure likely to close it, and the safest kind of traveller. The **home verse** joins that public warning to concealed water, refuge chambers, fungal beds, and escape routes. Singing it before anyone who has accepted no reciprocal duty is a serious betrayal. Company surveyors therefore receive forecasts whose practical instruction is sound and whose full geography is absent. Some evicted communities also leave stale refrains where Company listeners will hear them; among those communities this is route denial, not testimony, though Company reports rarely preserve the distinction.

An unfamiliar rune standard, sudden deep injury, or a route from which nobody returns can make a good song useless overnight. The honest response is an unfinished cadence and a closed road. When an institution demands a complete chart, its clerk often copies the last confident version into the gap. Later readers receive confidence that no singer performed.
