---
title: The Harrowind Coast
description: A candidate coastal culture choosing between discovering western waters and bringing familiar shores closer.
---

On the Harrowind Coast, people argue about the next great voyage while standing inside unfinished boats. A shipwright moves a bench to show how many passengers her design will carry. Her rival asks them to get out again to demonstrate how much weight his narrower hull saves. By launch day, half the town has acquired an opinion about a boat it cannot afford.

Harrowind is a chain of small, predominantly [[Humans|human]] port republics on a western coast. Wooded river valleys open between windy headlands; timber and hemp come downhill to boatyards, grain to smokehouses whose workers buy their supper on the way home. Fishing and coastal carrying support most households. Plenty of residents know every rock around their own cove and feel no urgent need for additional rocks.

The towns nevertheless save toward a common voyage, usually about once in a generation. Children perform the last expedition's quarrels in quay-side plays and compete for the navigator's part, which involves losing his trousers. Older sailors teach them to compare the wind at the harbour mouth with the water farther out. Remembering a famous landfall earns applause; noticing when to bring a small boat home earns another afternoon in it.

Private trials recur between common voyages. Crews borrow or hire working boats for a few days or weeks, testing a sail, a course beyond the headland, or a way of carrying more water. Fishing crews lend spare days, prosperous households buy canvas, and a persuasive stranger sometimes borrows a hull. After returning, crews spread damaged gear on the beach and answer questions. Support moves toward designs people have seen work. Boat owners can refuse another loan, and disappointed backers can provision a rival.

The common undertaking is much larger: a vessel fitted for months without a known harbour, substantial reserves, and enough paid crew to keep watches while people fall sick or make repairs. It must also replace earnings households lose during the absence. A few weeks in a borrowed fishing boat cannot establish that all this will work. Small trials fit around livelihoods; a long voyage consumes savings from harbour dues accumulated over decades. On returning, the common vessel earns its keep carrying coastal cargo and eventually wears out. The towns save for the next ambitious refit or replacement. A generation is an expectation, not a sailing date.

Adult residents choose their town's preferred voyage at an open harbour vote. A majority of towns selects the common proposal and its captain, but only willing towns pool their saved dues; too little backing postpones departure. Volunteers still decide whether to sail. The captain commands aboard, with an agreed deputy taking over immediately if needed and the crew choosing a lasting replacement. At home the captain has ordinary standing. Sailors can dispute the return account before the harbour gathering, and yards can refuse a disgraced captain's next commission. A popular song can make a quieter witness painfully difficult to hear.

Before departure, sailors visit [[Gods and Religion|shrines]] for favourable weather and safe return, approaching several gods according to need. [[Adventurers|Delvers]] sometimes join as guards or companions. Their first afternoon aboard usually restores everyone's sense of proportion.

Nessa, a pilot, wants to cross the uncharted western water. She loves the work of making an unfamiliar sea understandable: comparing bird flights, soundings and changes in the swell until another crew can follow her account. She hopes to find landfalls and return with sailing directions that ordinary Harrowind boats could eventually use. There may be no reachable shore. She wants the coast to spend enough time out there to learn something small trials cannot settle, even if the useful discovery is where to turn back.

Oren wants to reach a familiar distant coast and return in half the usual season. His fast boat once brought his wife home in time to see her dying sister; he imagines visits made for happier reasons, with time to stay. His trial crew sells model boats with room for passengers. Nessa calls his proposal a delivery run, then spends an evening helping him solve a steering fault. She enjoys the problem and resents how good his boat is becoming. Both ambitions need endurance trials beyond what their backers can presently afford.

- Nessa's best navigator has bought one of Oren's models and is asking whether a western landfall would ever become a journey her children could make.
- A small harbour backs Nessa, but its shipwright doubts either trial hull can carry the necessary reserves. She wants both rivals to help design the common vessel before the vote.
- A sailor from the previous voyage intends to interrupt its anniversary play with an account of why they really turned home.
