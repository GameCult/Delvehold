---
title: The Enamel Streets of Vey
description: A human craft town learning to make pictures larger than its customers can carry.
---

Sella holds a copper plaque with long tongs while her partner warms its underside. On the face, crushed blue glass loses its grainy appearance and begins to shine. He eases the heat away from a thin corner before it bends; she turns the plaque so the last dull patch faces him. They stop together. Yesterday's attempt had a fine sky and a curled-up riverbank.

This is how the enamel workers of Vey make their pictures: glass fused onto prepared metal, with a mage controlling the heat while another worker watches the surface. The little rivers and market awnings remain after the spell ends. Once cool, a plaque needs no further magic, though a fall can chip it badly.

Vey is a predominantly [[Humans|human]] town on a surface river, where orchard carts meet barges carrying metal and glass. Its enamel trade grew from decorated buckles and shop signs. Merchants began taking small street pictures home, and now customers arrive with drawings of places the makers have never seen. Between commissions, painters walk the town sketching things that visitors seldom buy: washing caught on a chimney, a barge being unloaded badly, the long backs of people shelling peas.

The heat work uses ordinary learned [[Mana and Pattern|spellcasting]]. Apprentices gather mana and shape a working under supervision; they first learn to close it safely, as in other traditions described by [[Runic Engineering]]. A steady effect is only the beginning. Thin copper heats differently from a heavy edge, and a change in the glass mixture can spoil a firing that went well yesterday. Makers learn from broken pieces, turn the work, and adjust the heat while there is still time. Their spells supply no missing glass and cannot make a cracked picture whole by warming it harder.

The workshops also use kilns for batches and broad grounds of colour. Close spellwork earns its expense on small corrections and demanding pieces. Some shops buy casting wands; others train mages to shape each working themselves. Neither purchase nor training supplies the eye that knows when to stop. The town's best painter cannot cast, and its busiest heat-worker paints birds that customers reliably identify as pears.

People learn enough about the work to have preferences. On summer evenings, finished plaques stand outside the shops where the low sun catches them. A successful blue draws people halfway down the street. There are buyers who love exact likenesses and others who ask for their house with a better roof, more flowers, or the hill moved slightly left. Sella enjoys these requests. Her partner Iven wants to paint the river as it looks from the muddy landing, including the mud.

Their present ambition is a picture along the public stair from that landing to the market. Each copper panel would be small enough to fire and replace separately; together they would show a single day on the river. Boat crews waiting for cargo could recognize their work at the bottom, then follow the painted water uphill. Several crews have already sat for sketches. One insists that the rope handling be corrected before anyone troubles about the faces.

Sella wants the stair picture bright enough to see through rain. Iven wants people to come close and discover details over years. Both have made persuasive sample panels. To work on the stair through the summer, they must turn down portable commissions that would pay them immediately. Other shops have offered help, but joining their pictures means agreeing how the river crosses each seam. Vey has many competent makers accustomed to finishing the whole sky themselves.

For now, visitors can buy small pictures of the proposed great picture. These sell quite well. Sella has begun to worry that they could spend a comfortable lifetime preparing to begin.

## Unresolved hooks

- A boat painter offers to pay for the first stair panels if the makers include the landing at dawn, when she works. Their design currently begins at noon.
- Iven's finest trial panel is subtle at arm's length and almost blank from the opposite stair. He wants it installed where people naturally pause; that is also the best place for Sella's brilliant blue.
- A returning customer wants twelve matching river pictures by autumn. Accepting would fund winter work, but consume the summer in which the boat crews can sit for them.
