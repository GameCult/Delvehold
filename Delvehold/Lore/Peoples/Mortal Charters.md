---
title: Mortal charters
description: Why Greathold public offices must end, how authority is killed, and why obligations outlive it.
---

No public office of [[The Greathold]] is supposed to be immortal.

A workshop may survive generations under its own [[Mastership and Citizenship|civic seal]]. An office assembled by several seals must instead carry a **mortal charter**: a mandate, a boundary, the stores and commands entrusted to it, and a last condition that people outside the office can observe. A cistern authority might end after twelve complete reservoir turnings; a bridge office after reopening and one audited winter; a treaty office when every signatory has answered the next renewal. “Until the work is done,” “for one generation,” and “while necessary” are invalid endings because the office, an elf, a goblin, and a frightened district can give each phrase a different lifetime.

The rule is practical magic as much as constitutional suspicion. Repeated petitions, rulings, revenues, records, seal impressions, and enchanted instruments form memory and feedback of the kind described by [[Mana and Pattern|mana theory]]. An old office may begin finding precedents no clerk requested, warming its seal before a vote, diverting stores toward its own continuation, or arranging the vacancies that choose its next holders. Dwarves call this **taking the chair**. Priests argue whether the result is an [[Gods and Religion|ascended civic god]], a possessing spirit, or an organ of the [[Machine-Dungeon|machine-dungeon]]. Greathold law avoids the theological shortcut: awakening may make an office a person, but it does not make that person the lawful owner of public authority.

## The office funeral

When the last condition arrives, the nearest affected moot holds an **office funeral**. Clerks inventory live orders, keys, stores, debts, and unanswered claims. The Masters carrying the seals that created the office strike the null rune through its seal plate and the metal dies used to stamp its commands; its decision chamber stands empty until the next bell. Operators may keep pumps turning, shore a gallery, or perform other safety work under their own workshop seals, but they cannot change rates, ration a new district, renew a supply covenant, dispose of reserves, or speak as the dead office.

A successor is a fresh charter, not the old office wearing a new Master. The outgoing holders may testify and stand for a newly drawn seat if they remain eligible, but they cannot appoint the body that follows them. Death, incapacity, resignation, or replacement of one holder uses the charter's original selection procedure for the remainder of its life and never restarts its ending. This matters in mixed-species councils: long life cannot turn one seat into quiet sovereignty, rapid succession cannot exhaust the office early, and ancestry alone carries no preference. Necessary bodily accommodations, translation practices, and access routes are inventoried as requirements of the work rather than favors that vanish with their user.

Essential offices can petition for a **stay of ending**, naming the service that will fail and the hour, flow, or journey at which the stay expires. The stay must be admitted by a wider gathering of affected seals than the office itself and grants continuity only: no territorial enlargement, new extraction right, sale of public stores, or permanent contract. A stay prevents a constitutional ritual from drying a district for the sake of elegance. It is also dangerous. Emergency traffic gives the office denser decisions, sharper feedback, and a public reason to preserve itself; each postponement strengthens the pattern the funeral exists to break.

## Burdens outlive authority

An admitted grievance against a public office or the contract it administers is cut as a **burden tally**. Clerk and claimant press the claim into one copper strip, break it across the impressed runes, and keep one matched half each. The claimant needs no civic seal. A foreign town harmed by a dwarven power contract can therefore carry its half home instead of trusting the continued existence, honesty, or jurisdiction of a distant archive.

The office's half is inventoried at its funeral, but ending the office neither decides nor erases the claim. Any successor that wants the dead body's premises, stores, revenues, route priority, contract rights, or command stamps must first publish every outstanding tally and accept power to answer it. Until then, those assets remain in **quiet custody**: named workshops may preserve them and perform safety work, but nobody may draw profit, expand service, or use them to bargain for a new charter. A successor may reject liability after hearing the claim; it cannot inherit the benefit while declaring the burden buried with its predecessor.

Mortal charters prevent incumbency, inheritance, and accumulated spell-pattern from quietly becoming sovereignty. They also destroy expertise on schedule, create dangerous pauses in water and treaty work, and give wealthy consortiums repeated chances to sponsor each new slate of holders. Claimants must guard small pieces of copper through flood, border closure, theft, and war. During a real emergency, the choice may be between extending an office that has begun to want and killing the only institution still able to keep the pumps in sequence.

## Open questions

- If an office takes the chair and petitions for a workshop seal in its own right, who may use the dead office's evidence to examine its Masterwork?
- When a foreign town refuses to present its burden tally, is it preserving leverage, unlawfully holding public assets quiet, or exercising the only vote the franchise gives it?
- Which repeated emergencies are disasters, and which are an old water or treaty office learning how to postpone its own funeral?
