title: Halflings
description: Early beneficiaries of industrial magic whose farms become evidence of the spreading crisis.
---

Halfling communities are concentrated in fertile agricultural regions and trading settlements. They benefit early and visibly from dwarven industry: enchanted pumps, refrigeration, preservation runes, improved mills, mechanical harvesters, and cheap light.

Refrigeration keeps grain through a wet summer. Enchanted pumps replace the old hauling gangs, and local workshops buy work once monopolized by distant mages.

## The turn

Opposition grows when underground instability reaches soil and weather. Mana contamination brings crop mutation, magical pests, livestock abnormalities, untimely storms, and localized ecological collapse.

Agricultural communities are excellent observers of slow environmental change because livelihood depends on remembering what a field did ten years ago. Their records supply politically awkward evidence that deep extraction produces effects far beyond dwarven borders.

Radical meetings begin when the pump still works but its irrigated field grows glassy weeds. Campaigns demand a different crystal supply and restitution from the mine; few volunteer to restore the hauling gangs.

## The Glasswater Clause at Mallowfen

*Material-economy elaboration authored for the Ghostlight fixture `ledger-glasswater-clause-v0`.*

Mallowfen's Nine-Reed Water Fellowship bought its first lifting engine from Copper Mantle, a Greathold [[Dwarven Industry|forge consortium]], on a seven-harvest lease. The fellowship's member farms owe one joint instalment at the first grain auction of each year. Copper Mantle keeps title to the pump and its standardized governor plate until the last payment; a trained Mallowfen workshop may clean channels, replace bearings, and certify pressure, but breaking the plate's seal ends the guarantee. The guarantee also requires numbered crystal lots bought through the consortium's factors. A cheaper or differently sourced crystal may keep the water moving and still make every later loss the buyer's problem.

The human Charterhouse of the Third Bell underwrites the lease. Its policy covers a cracked pump body, a seized lift, and a breached masonry cistern, because each leaves a stoppage an assessor can measure. Crop mutation, altered soil, new pests, and water that acquires a harmful pattern after passing the certified delivery lip are excluded unless the claimant proves a mechanical fault inside the engine. Mallowfen borrowers call this exclusion the **Glasswater Clause**: if clear water crosses the lip at the contracted height and rate, the pump has legally succeeded even when the first irrigated furrow grows translucent roots.

That division of risk binds the district tighter than ownership would. A contaminated harvest does not suspend the instalment. If Nine-Reed defaults, Copper Mantle may repossess the engine before the next sowing; if the fellowship stops using it without an admitted mechanical fault, the Charterhouse may cancel both loss cover and the emergency-water rider. Running the pump protects the contract and may spread the injury. Stopping it protects the fields and can forfeit the machine they require to survive a dry month. Diverting suspect water into the old stone holding beds buys evidence and time, but starves the millcourse and spends the district's cistern reserve. The contract turns uncertainty about [[Mana and Pattern|patterned mana]] into a debt paid first by households least able to wait for the [[Surface Symptoms|international argument]] to settle.
