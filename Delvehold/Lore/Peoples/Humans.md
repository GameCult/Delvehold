Human societies include kingdoms, republics, city-states, merchant leagues, principalities, and empires. Their fragmentation makes a single “human position” impossible.

Human cities buy dwarven pumps, lights, rails, weapons, and power systems. A rail contract creates a constituency for open crystal trade, ties public transport to a foreign supplier, and takes municipal work from local mage guilds. The same parliament then hears all three interests when relations with the Greathold worsen.

### Mossgate bellhouses

*Authored elaboration for the Ghostlight fixture `hearth-mossgate-bellhouse-v0`.*

Mossgate is a human market town whose dwarven-built rail spur carries orchard produce and whose imported crystal schedule also drives the irrigation pumps. Houses nearest the station keep an old local obligation called the bellhouse watch. If the returning bell fails, a bellhouse collects named children from the platform school, checks neighbours who depend on pumped water or the station lift, feeds stranded workers, and gives repair crews somewhere to sleep. The station roster recognizes the houses but does not command them; belonging is proved by answering the silent bell often enough that other tables answer for yours.

Foreign apprentices lodged with these households are commonly called **table-kin**. The name grants no inheritance or Greathold civic seal. It does create reciprocal expectations: the household supplies a bed, meals, local introductions, and care through illness; the apprentice contributes wages when able, takes a share of domestic work, and answers the bellhouse watch even when the railway calls the same hands to an engine fault. Many apprenticeships become kin relationships in practice. Others remain cheap company housing with affectionate language laid over the invoice.

The railway operator releases station emergency stores and compensates registered bellhouses only after it records an outage. A stopped line may instead be entered as a service pause while household pantries, water jars, and missed shifts carry the first night. Long closures separate wage earners from children, interrupt produce and medicine, and exhaust the homes feeding the repair crews needed to restore service. Wealthier districts hire carts and private cisterns; the platform wards become more crowded precisely as their means shrink. In Mossgate, an argument about crystal imports reaches parliament after it has already been settled provisionally at somebody's kitchen table.

## The battleground of interpretation

Universities become clearing houses for evidence the interested parties cannot settle themselves. A disputed survey can arrive with dwarven company restrictions, an elven model, and funding from a threatened mage guild. Its publication may change import law before the underlying argument is resolved.

Some states restrict core products, deep-mined crystals, dungeon tissues, and dwarven engines. Such policies may be genuine precaution, economic protectionism, strategic decoupling, or all three at once.

## Intervention

Human containment plans eventually target factories and mana depots. The same strike that interrupts deep extraction can stop a civilian rail or pumping station fed by the network. Military communiqués list the intended target; casualty reports list everything connected to it.
