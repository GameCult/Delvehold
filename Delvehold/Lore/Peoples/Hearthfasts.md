---
title: Hearthfasts
description: Household memory that accumulates in leased spell engines and turns maintenance into a kinship crisis.
---

A **hearthfast** is a household whose repeated life has become part of the runic infrastructure sustaining it. The same word names the retained magical pattern and the people, rooms, tools, and engines caught inside it.

Hearthfasts are most common in foreign towns that bought dwarven pumps, lifts, refrigeration, heating, or local rail control from the [[Dwarven Industry|Forge Consortiums]]. They also occur throughout [[The Greathold]], especially where one old array has served several generations or species. Their existence is disputed but not mysterious: [[Mana and Pattern|mana carries the history of the runes it has passed through]], and mature [[Runic Engineering|spell engines]] retain feedback so they can compare present demand with previous failures.

## How a household enters the engine

A domestic engine receives the same small irregularities for years. The night baker draws heat before the district wakes. A [[Goblins|goblin]] child needs the washroom kept humid through a moult. A dwarf with an injured knee takes longer to clear the lift gate. A [[Humans|human]] midwife keeps one cistern branch above its nominal reserve. Technicians solve each case by adjusting correction plates, thresholds, and return paths rather than rebuilding the engine.

Eventually the corrections cease to behave like separate settings. The array anticipates combinations of gait, mana trace, timing, tools, rooms, and repeated acts of care. It warms the bench before the injured resident sits, delays a door for the person carrying supper upstairs, and saves water because someone has begun laying out the birthing cloths. No bloodline is privileged. A fosterling, lodger, apprentice, former enemy, or cross-species partner becomes part of the pattern by being cared for and learning to care in return.

Households call this being **held by the hearth**. It is not proof that the engine has a private mind. It is proof that the household now exists partly outside its members' bodies and recollections. In neighborhoods where a registry omitted a migrant, an adoption was never sealed, or a dead Master kept the only apprenticeship papers, the old engine's accommodations may be the last surviving witness that someone belonged.

The memory lies chiefly in feedback and correction plates, but also in the joined conduits and practiced maintenance gestures around them. A competent apprentice may know which harmless-looking deviation lets a grandparent sleep through winter. That knowledge travels even less cleanly than the model-specific craft already criticized in [[Runic Engineering#Displacement|technician training]]. Apprentices therefore become household kin in fact long before [[Mastership and Citizenship|Mastership law]] gives them an independent civic voice.

## The clean-hearth reset

Consortium manuals call a hearthfast **domestic retention** or **compound drift**. Insurers normally require a **clean-hearth reset** after transfer of a lease, a major covered failure, prolonged nonpayment, or service by an unauthorized technician. The authorized crew removes the feedback plates, flushes retained mana from the serviceable conduits, and reinstalls the standard graph. Removed plates travel under seal to the owner for failure analysis because the warranty treats them as part of the leased engine, not as household property.

The rule protects a real invariant. A deeply retained engine may spend extra fuel on familiar bodies, conceal a failing branch that residents have learned to live around, or refuse a rationing command because it predicts harm to someone it has always protected. Linked hearthfasts could also be the smallest domestic tissue of the emerging [[Machine-Dungeon|machine-dungeon]]. Public infrastructure cannot quietly grant one remembered household authority over every stranger on the same line.

The reset is nevertheless an amputation. Afterwards the lift closes at its catalogued speed, the heat follows the standard dwarven sleep cycle, and the cistern no longer recognizes an unregistered sickroom. Mixed-species households suffer first because standard settings encode the bodies for which the engine was sold. When a foreign rail contract collapses or a border closes, families must choose between an aging, uninsured hearthfast no authorized crew will touch and a replacement engine that treats everyone as a new tenant.

Households call an involuntary reset **hearth-stripping**. Some hide retention plates before repossession; some require a departing child to memorize the engine's quirks; some refuse safer replacements because the obsolete array still waits for a missing person. Forge Consortiums describe these acts as warranty fraud and awakening risk. Both descriptions can be true.

## Open pressures

- When a retention plate reconstructs a missing resident after repeated authorized resets, is it evidence, haunting, concealed engineering, or refusal by an emerging person?
- If a household flees across a border, who may carry the plates that remember it: the equipment owner, the people whose care formed the pattern, or the apprentice who can still read it?
- What does a neighborhood owe when its hearthfast protects one member by rationing heat, water, or transport away from people it never learned to recognize?
