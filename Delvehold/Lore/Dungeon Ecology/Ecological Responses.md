---
title: Ecological responses
description: How dungeon systems identify, resist, and learn from industrial disturbance.
---

The first responses to deep extraction look like ordinary mine failures: collapses, strange migration, shifting veins, waking local cores, and equipment loss. Each incident admits a comfortable explanation. Coordination makes comfort harder.

## Escalation pattern

1. **Avoidance:** mana deposits retreat and passages seal.
2. **Local defence:** creatures attack pumps, rails, substations, containment arrays, and drills while ignoring nearby homes.
3. **Specialization:** moulds consume mana leakage; insects break standardized runes; burrowers seek crystal stores.
4. **Coordination:** distant dungeons repeat architectural and behavioural changes without a visible connection.
5. **Systemic response:** monster migrations, mana storms, dungeon blooms, and geological disturbance spread beyond extraction zones.

## Competing names

Mining companies call the pattern **deep mana instability**. Engineers prefer **adaptive geomantic feedback**. Militaries report **subterranean hostile activity**. Elven observers describe injury and immune response. Goblin communities use more practical language: the tunnels know what the machines are for.

Names allocate responsibility. Instability invites better stabilization; hostility invites weapons; injury invites restraint or repair. None of the names is neutral.

## Intelligence

Local adaptation, shared mana currents, nested cores, goblin intelligence networks, and awakened minds can converge without a single commander. Whatever the mixture, attacks pass nearby homes to reach pumps and substations, then change when those machines are redesigned.

Stronger wards demand more mana and protect larger drills. The next response arrives adapted to both.
