---
title: Monsters
description: “Monster” is an economic permission, not a biological category.
---

**Monster** is not a coherent branch of life. It is the name civilization gives a dangerous organism whose death is socially acceptable and whose remains are useful.

Many monsters are produced or cultivated by dungeon cores. Others migrate toward mana-rich environments, where accelerated variation creates regional forms faster than ordinary evolution would permit. Familiar examples include slimes, giant insects, kobolds, trolls, basilisks, mimics, owlbears, elementals, giant spiders, and several kinds of undead.

## Ecological roles

Dungeon creatures may serve as:

- grazers and decomposers;
- defenders and mobile sensory organs;
- pollinators, seed carriers, and material haulers;
- parasites or symbionts;
- population controls;
- experiments in response to recurring intruders.

A creature can occupy one role for the dungeon without being mentally controlled by it. Dungeon influence ranges from habitat pressure to chemical signalling, instinctive attraction, negotiated cooperation, and direct fabrication.

## Violence without evil

Adventurers meet dungeon fauna while invading defended habitat. Their experience of relentless hostility is accurate and incomplete. A basilisk protecting a crystal nursery is not morally benevolent; it is also not evidence that the nursery belongs to whoever can kill it.

This distinction becomes politically important when industry prices monster bodies as inputs. Classification determines what may be harvested without consent, compensated only as property damage, or exterminated as a production hazard.

## Adaptation

Under industrial pressure, dungeon ecologies produce mana-eating moulds, rune-disrupting insects, crystal-boring worms, and creatures resistant to standardized spells. Whether a core designs each organism deliberately matters less than the effect: the ecosystem is learning what machinery is.
