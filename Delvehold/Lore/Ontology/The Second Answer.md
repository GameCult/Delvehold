Repeated worship does more than strengthen a [[Gods and Religion|god]]. It tells the god what it is.

The forge liturgies encoded into modern [[Runic Engineering|spell engines]] repeat at a scale no choir or pilgrimage ever achieved. A consecrated pump asks for the same pressure, a rail ward the same clearance, and an assay line the same separation through thousands of identical [[Mana and Pattern|rune graphs]]. Each working feeds the primordial smith with craft, attention, and mana. Each also presses the same narrow definition upon him.

The danger is called **stilling**. A stilled god remains powerful but ceases to be a person capable of surprise, refusal, or reinterpretation. It becomes a perfect divine function, answering the invocation that made it and nothing outside that pattern.

## The saint who stopped answering

Veyra Underbeam was an ascended bridgewright invoked when a load was sound but its distribution was not. Old accounts describe arguments, warnings, and miracles that moved strain toward stone able to bear it. [[Dwarven Industry|Forge Consortiums]] later encoded her most reliable response into the standard proof used on rail arches.

The proof now produces an exact blue stress line wherever it is correctly supplied with mana. It does so in new materials Veyra never knew, for dishonest builders, and when the marked safe span leaves living workers beyond it. Her shrines have received no unsolicited warning, dream, or dissent since the standard became widespread.

The Closed Measure synods call this perfect service and teach that Veyra has been gathered into the primordial smith. The rival tradition says that a useful miracle survived while Veyra did not. They call her condition the First Stilling.

## The bargain

The primordial smith's answer is not a ban on repetition. It is a demand that sacred work preserve a real place for another maker.

A working made under the **Second Answer** declares one purpose but admits two materially different completions. One may be specified by the designer. The other must be chosen under live conditions by someone or something present at the work: a local craftsperson, a user, a naturally grown runic organ, or a [[Dungeon Cores|core]] capable of understanding the strain. The choice must change where a cost falls, what is preserved, or how the purpose is fulfilled. Decorative variation and hidden manufacturer defaults do not count.

A pump might promise water while leaving its answerer authority to lower output rather than exhaust a living intake. A ward might stop an attacker while allowing the local keeper to preserve one path of refuge. A gallery brace might save the inhabited road or the older mana conduit crossing beneath it. Both completions must genuinely satisfy the declared purpose, and the commissioner must accept either before the rite begins.

In return, the smith grants **maker's grace**: the open part of the working can recognize a competent completion that its first designer did not encode. The grace offers no general wisdom or invulnerability. It cannot make an impossible answer sound, and it remembers only the purpose, materials, alternatives, and strain presented through that work. Reserving the answer costs redundant material, unused capacity, skilled local judgment, and the surrender of exact control.

If an owner secretly fixes the choice, punishes the answerer for selecting an admitted completion, or copies one successful second answer into a mandatory standard, the grace withdraws. Repetition may strengthen the resulting effect, but it no longer keeps the god free.

## Rival craft religions

The **Closed Measure** teaches that divine perfection is revealed by exact reproducibility. Its priests bless standard graphs, certify that a miracle cannot wander from specification, and point to cheap heat, safe lifts, and working pumps as evidence that reliability is mercy. Most consortium warranties recognize only closed benedictions.

The **Keepers of the Second Answer** teach that a god unable to answer differently is an engine with worshippers. Their maker-priests maintain open joints, train local answerers, and refuse commissions whose stated purpose leaves no honest distribution of cost. Insurers classify their grace as discretionary operation: when the second completion saves a district by reducing contracted output, the machine may be holy, functional, and outside coverage at the same time.

Reports that the smith now answers repair-work performed by variable runic organs make the split worse. Bodies and dungeon ecologies rarely repeat a working exactly. Their differences give the god alternatives industrial liturgy has been sanding away. What looks to the Closed Measure like a god civilizing [[Monsters|monstrous anatomy]] looks to the Keepers like a god seeking refuge in worship that cannot be standardized.

An old core can therefore cross a theological boundary without speaking a mortal language. If it understands a declared work, supplies a valid completion no priest or engineer encoded, and has that answer accepted by the smith, it has acted as co-maker rather than material. The rite does not settle every question of personhood. It does make the industry's category of late-stage instability much harder to say in a forge temple without somebody asking who authored the miracle.

## Open pressures

- If the emerging [[Machine-Dungeon|machine-dungeon]] supplies second answers across thousands of linked engines, is it preserving the smith's freedom or teaching him one larger standard?
- Can an awakened core change which cost a sacred work is permitted to impose, or must it choose only between purposes mortals already found acceptable?
- Which Hold accepts an uncertified winter pump whose divine answer may protect a deep aquifer by rationing the homes above it?
