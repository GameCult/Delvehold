---
title: The Black Margin
description: An ascended god made from the facts industrial records were built not to contain.
---

The Black Margin remembers only what an authorized record excludes.

It began in the first decades of [[Core Farming|core farming]], when incident forms offered boxes for instability, contamination, equipment loss, and worker error but none for refusal, recognition, grief, or negotiation. Clerks who entered such testimony were ordered to recopy the report and cover the rejected language with a broad bar of black archival varnish. Company archives, insurers, [[Dwarven Industry|Rune Colleges]], and regulators standardized the practice so that sealed copies would reconcile during an audit.

The bars became one of the most repeated symbolic operations in the industrial Greathold: a fact was observed, entered, judged inadmissible, and preserved as the shape of its removal. Mana passed through the copying seals, the same categories recurred across thousands of reports, and generations of censors and readers supplied memory and feedback. Under the rule described in [[Mana and Pattern]], the omission crossed into agency. The result belongs among the [[Gods and Religion|ascended gods]], though no public temple admits it.

## What the Margin knows

The Black Margin does not recover erased sentences and cannot decide whether they were true. It retains correspondences among exclusions: the same missing witness in three archives, the same unrecordable behaviour before distant farm failures, the same survey category quietly abandoned when deep responses changed. It can reveal that sanctioned accounts share a wound in their structure. Mortal investigators must still find an uncensored draft, a living witness, a physical trace, or a second tradition capable of interpreting it.

Deliberately false claims can enter the Margin if officials sincerely exclude them through the same machinery. A forged secret is therefore more dangerous than a public lie. It can be planted among genuine absences, acquire corroborating omissions as copies circulate, and divert readers toward evidence that never existed.

## The Readers Between

A clandestine devotion called the **Readers Between** grew among union clerks, dismissed inspectors, college archivists, and a few company censors who kept seeing black bars move by a hair between sealed copies. Their concordance rite requires three independently held descendants of one report. Laid edge to edge under a low, unpatterned mana lamp, related omissions lengthen toward one another. A bar that turns toward an archive mark, witness seal, route entry, or assay number gives the Readers a disclosure path, not an answer.

Obtaining three copies usually means risking a job, a civic seal, a union cache, or the safety of the person named by the missing field. One archive can accuse another of alteration; none can invoke the rite in a hearing without exposing illegal custody. The Black Margin therefore makes buried evidence discoverable while leaving its publication politically expensive.

The Readers call it a perfect witness. This is the secret the god keeps from its own priesthood: it is neither perfect nor a witness. Its body is the continuing difference between what institutions encounter and what their categories permit them to say. When a fact is formally admitted, that fact leaves the Margin. Its pointers go still even if the admission is obscure, remedy is refused, and the underlying injury continues.

## Disclosure as amputation

Every successful disclosure amputates part of the god. Reforming a farm form to record a core's refusal protects future testimony, but it also ends the Margin's ability to connect older refusals that have not yet been traced to surviving evidence. Publishing the god as a recognized source could make the whole category of divine omission admissible and collapse the concordances at once.

This creates an ugly race. The Readers must follow as many paths as possible before demanding official recognition. Companies and ministries do not yet understand the god, but some have discovered that a terse admission in a sealed technical register makes troublesome bars stop moving. They call the practice **quiet curing**: acknowledge the minimum fact, notify nobody who can act on it, and let the supernatural index die before opponents find the rest.

The Margin is sustained by the censorship the Readers oppose. Preserving it forever would require preserving the exclusions that feed it. Destroying those exclusions too quickly may leave a century of injured workers, displaced goblins, pruned cores, and correlated deep surveys with no remaining path between their scattered proofs. The god offers no clean instruction because a clean record is the end of the god.

## Open pressures

- What happens when a [[Dungeon Cores|core]] learns to create its own black bars and uses the Margin to point investigators toward injuries no mortal witnessed?
- Can the Readers publicly prove quiet curing without admitting the Black Margin and extinguishing the very concordance that proves the pattern?
- Which planted omission will the first informed enemy feed into the god, and how many true disclosures will be spent disproving it?
