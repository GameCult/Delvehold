---
title: Nested dungeons
description: Local cores are organs and inhabitants of larger magical ecologies.
---

A dungeon can be part of a larger dungeon system while remaining active in its own right. Its local core may serve as an organ within a mountain intelligence, and that mountain may belong to still larger systems.

Surface creatures usually encounter only an entrance, a defensible territory, and perhaps one material core. From there, a shallow dungeon looks like a separate place. Deeper connections reveal systems that overlap at several scales:

**local core → regional core → deep core → continental core → planetary core**

This is an approximate hierarchy of scale. It does not describe a chain of command.

A cave core, for example, may feed a fungal network that regulates one slope of a mountain intelligence. Snowmelt connects the mountain to a watershed, and a continental mana current crosses both. These systems affect one another without any one of them commanding the rest. Their borders shift with season, pressure, damage, and memory.

## Nested identity

Belonging to a larger system does not make every inhabitant share its purposes. A goblin community inside a dungeon keeps its own aims. The dungeon remains active within a mountain intelligence, while the mountain participates in continental mana currents and the [[World-Spirit]]. These relationships do not settle where one will begins or another ends.

Elven spiritual thought has long had words for this kind of nesting. Industrial property law has a different problem: it needs boundaries that claims can be measured against. A boundary stone can mark a claim, but roots and remembered injuries extend across it.

[[The Greathold]] is the setting's largest inhabited example. Each Hold occupies a Vault formed and maintained by a local core. Roads, rails, and civic lifts connect the Holds along older structures. The local cores remain active within the mountain-range system, even where dwarves have built their settlements. The inhabitants have changed the world-dungeon and continue to live within it.

## Practical consequence

A mine lease can enclose the place where miners cut without enclosing everything their work affects. A bore may intersect tissue that serves a much wider dungeon system.

Repeated disturbance travels through mana currents. Distant dungeons then begin to show identical changes, even where no visible tunnel connects them. This pattern emerges during the [[Deep Mining|deep-mining crisis]]: the effects of excavation extend beyond the workings and the boundaries drawn around them.
