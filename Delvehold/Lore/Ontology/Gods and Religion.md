---
title: Gods and religion
description: Celestial, chthonic, and ascended divinities within a world of persistent magical patterns.
---

The gods are real. Their relationship to dungeon ontology remains disputed, including among the gods' own institutions.

## Celestial gods

Celestial gods gather around abstract domains: justice, war, commerce, death, love, knowledge, kingship, storms, and craft. They are less geographically bounded than dungeon minds. Worship, ritual, symbolic association, and institutional continuity increase their power.

Churches teach that their gods precede worship. The possibility that worship stabilizes or even generates divinity is doctrinally explosive, not conclusively settled.

## Chthonic gods

Mountain, river, forest, sea, cavern, and volcano spirits are often awakened ecological systems interpreted through religious relationship. Elven theologians consider this unsurprising. Dwarven theologians consider the reduction of an honoured god to “a dungeon” both crude and backwards: the ontology may instead be expanding the category of person.

## Ascended gods

Saints, ancestral rulers, heroes, archmages, and great monsters occasionally establish durable divine identities. If sufficient magical continuity produces agency, ascension may be a symbolic pattern becoming self-maintaining across minds and mana.

## Ordinary religion

Most people are practical pluralists. Farmers pray for harvest, soldiers for survival, merchants for honest scales or profitable dishonesty, dwarven smiths invoke the Forge Father, and funeral priests guide the dead. A person may honour several cults without feeling a contradiction.

The ontological crisis therefore begins among engineers, scholars, priests, and political elites. The average miner does not require a unified theory of divinity. They require the tunnel to remain above their head.

## Divine politics

Industrialization divides revelation. Earth cults report pain under the mountains. Forge priests dream of dark furnaces. Gods of commerce favour abundant power; wilderness gods demand restraint; war gods prosper as the dispute militarizes. Divine institutions have interests, dependencies, and partial knowledge. No revelation ends the argument cleanly.
